# Reading modes

## Initial interpretation
Explain the core problem, changed mechanism, strongest evidence and limitations. Give concrete reading locations and questions. Do not claim full reading if only a first pass was performed.

## Full deep read
Inspect full relevant method, experiments and appendices; follow a running example through training and inference. Prioritize details that affect understanding or conclusions. Cover core claims even when evidence is negative; disclose access gaps.

## Equation or figure
Answer the local question first; read sufficient surrounding definitions. Explain what the equation computes and why, dimensions/assumptions, and its role. For figures identify axes, controls, comparisons and what the plot cannot establish. Do not infer unreported numerical values from visual impressions.

## Evidence audit
Map central claims to evidence, conditions and unsupported extensions. Distinguish absent evidence from evidence of absence. Avoid performative skepticism: retain supported positive findings.

## Implementation relevance
Recover interfaces, data/target construction, training and inference steps, latency/control dependencies and missing settings. Distinguish verified paper details, repository facts, and suggested engineering choices. Do not present suggested implementation as the authors' code.

## Named-paper comparison
Apply shared definitions to each named work. State non-comparable settings before judging results. Compare mechanisms and evidence rather than raw success rates across different tasks.

## Teaching
Start with intuition and one concrete task. Add formalism at the step where it becomes necessary. Use a small worked example only when mathematically sound and label it synthetic. End with a specific self-check question only if useful, not as a forced quiz.
